## The Five Gaps

### GAP 1: Zero Users
**Target close date:** 30 days from App Store approval

**Why it matters to investors:**
No user in hand = no proof the community will trust a safety app from an unknown founder. Community trust is earned, not assumed. One investor said it best: "In this space, users are your reference letters."

**The fix — in order of leverage:**

#### Step 1: Community Org Partnerships (highest leverage, do first)
These orgs already have the trust you need to borrow. One recommendation from CHIRLA reaches more qualified users than 10,000 Instagram impressions.

**Target orgs for LA launch:**
| Org | Why | How to approach |
|-----|-----|-----------------|
| CHIRLA (Coalition for Humane Immigrant Rights) | Largest immigrant rights org in CA. Serves tens of thousands of families. | Reach out through their advocacy@chirla.org — frame as a free tool for their clients |
| CARECEN-LA (Central American Resource Center) | Trusted with legal aid for Central American community | Email info@carecen-la.org — offer a private demo session for their staff |
| Homeboy Industries | Deep trust in Latino community, Father Greg Boyle has massive reach | Community partner request through homeboy.net/contact |
| Clergy and Laity United for Economic Justice (CLUE) | Network of 500+ congregations in LA | Request to present at a weekly clergy call |
| Promise Zone Coalition | East LA community org, hyper-local | Attend their monthly community meeting |

**Script to use when reaching out:**
> "We built a free safety app for the Latino community — no account, no email, completely anonymous. If someone is stopped, their family gets their location instantly. We're looking for trusted partners to help us get it into the right hands before we launch publicly. Can we show it to you over Zoom?"

#### Step 2: Soft Launch to 50 Beta Testers (before App Store approval)
- Use TestFlight while awaiting App Store review
- Recruit 50 people personally — focus on: community org staff, church leaders, immigration lawyers, LA-based Latino professionals
- Goal: generate 10–15 genuine testimonials in writing before public launch
- Ask each: "Would you recommend this to your family?"

#### Step 3: App Store + Organic Word of Mouth
- Primary distribution: community org QR codes in waiting rooms, at events
- Secondary: bilingual social posts from community org accounts (not your account — theirs)
- No paid ads at launch — paid installs don't validate trust, word-of-mouth does

**KPI:** 500 MAU within 30 days of App Store approval

### GAP 2: No Revenue Signal
**Target close date:** 60 days from launch

**Why it matters:**
Angels and accelerators want to see that at least some people are willing to pay — even $1 — because it proves the value prop is real, not just emotional.

**The fix — "Founding Member" program:**

Launch a voluntary "Founding Member" tier at **$3/month** before you build any premium features. Frame it as:

> "Acción is free — and will always be free — for everyone who needs it. Founding Members help us keep the servers running and build what comes next."

**What Founding Members get:**
- Name in the app's credits (under Settings → About)
- Early access to new features
- Direct line to the founder (a Telegram group or email thread)
- The knowledge that they helped build something for la comunidad

**Why this works:**
- No technical work required — just add a "Support Acción" button in Settings that links to Stripe or a direct App Store subscription
- Even 170 people paying $3/month = $510 MRR — you've crossed the $500 signal
- These people become your first vocal advocates. They have skin in the game.

**How to pitch it:**
Launch on the same day as the App Store — don't wait for users to find it. Email every one of your beta testers from TestFlight personally asking them to become Founding Members.

**KPI:** $500 MRR within 60 days

### GAP 3: No Traction Proof
**Target close date:** 60–90 days from launch

**Why it matters:**
"500 MAU" is table stakes for angels. But the story behind the number matters more: is it organic? Is it community-driven? Is retention high?

**The metrics that matter most (and what to track):**
| Metric | Target | Why it signals PMF |
|--------|--------|--------------------|
| DAU/MAU ratio | >20% | People come back regularly = real utility |
| SOS activations | Any real ones | Proves people use it in actual need, not just curiosity |
| Organic install source | >70% from word of mouth | Can't fake community trust |
| 30-day retention | >40% | Safety apps need to stick |
| Know Your Rights views | High relative to installs | Shows ambient daily utility |

**How to generate organic traction fast:**

1. **One story from a real user.** If even one person uses the SOS and their family finds them because of it — that story, told with their permission, is worth 10,000 downloads. Actively look for it. Ask your beta users to tell you when it helps.

2. **Embed in community org workflows.** If CHIRLA staff recommends it to every new client as standard intake, you get 50–100 installs/week passively. That's 200–400 MAU from one org.

3. **Instagram story campaigns.** Partner with 3–5 community org Instagram accounts. Ask them to share a simple post: "We recommend this app for safety. It's free. Link in bio." Even a 50K-follower org drives 500–1000 clicks.

4. **QR codes at events.** LA has weekly community events — health fairs, legal aid clinics, church outreach. A simple bilingual flyer with a QR code + one sentence of explanation is enough.

**KPI:** 500 MAU, >20% DAU/MAU, documented word-of-mouth install source

### GAP 4: No Team Narrative
**Target close date:** Before any pitch meeting

**Why it matters:**
At pre-seed, investors are mostly betting on the founder. The product is too early to validate on its own. Your story is your pitch.

**The Acción founder narrative (draft — make it yours):**

Three things need to be true for a founder story to land with investors:
1. **Why you** — lived proximity to the problem
2. **Why now** — timing isn't coincidence
3. **Why this** — you built something specific, not generic

**Draft founder story for pitches:**
> "I'm a 2x founder and product designer. First generation Mexican American. I've watched people in my community — people who are citizens, who were born here — live in fear of something that shouldn't touch them. When I started building what would become Acción, I thought I was building a hardware wearable. Then I realized the most urgent version of this problem doesn't need hardware — it needs a phone and a button. So that's what I built first. The software is live. The hardware is the roadmap. And I'm the person who's been close enough to this problem my whole life to build the right version of it."

**What to add to make it even stronger:**
- A specific moment — the personal story or person that made you start building
- A connection to the community org ecosystem (have you met with CHIRLA? Say that.)
- The product designer + founder combination — rare pairing, investors value it

**Where your story works best:**
- In person over Zoom with angels (they invest in people)
- Opening paragraph of every accelerator application
- Twitter/LinkedIn presence — start posting about building Acción now, before you raise. Social proof of builder + mission before the raise makes the raise easier.

### GAP 5: No Press
**Target close date:** 60 days from launch

**Why it matters:**
A single article in the LA Times or a Univision segment does three things: (1) validates your mission to investors, (2) drives installs, (3) opens doors to angels who would otherwise never see your cold email.

**How founders in the immigrant tech/safety space have gotten coverage:**

| App/Product | Press they got | How they got it |
|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Notifica** (United We Dream, 2017) | NYT, Vox, BuzzFeed News, Univision | Launched to existing org network, then one reporter (Roque Planas at HuffPost) was already covering ICE and reached out. Built the story around the human moment, not the technology. |
| **RAP (Rapid Response Network)** | Local LA news, NPR | Launched at a press conference alongside immigration attorneys. Made themselves quotable as experts, not just a product |
| **Palante** (immigration app) | TechCrunch, Univision | Connected with Latino tech journalists first — pitched the product as a follow-up on a story about tech for immigrant communities that the reporter had already written |
| **Cleared** (background check app for immigrants) | Forbes, LA Times | Found a reporter who had written about financial inclusion for immigrants in the past year and pitched a direct follow-up angle |

**The playbook that works:**

**Step 1: Find reporters who already care (not cold pitch)**
Search: "[reporter name] + ICE" or "[reporter name] + immigrant community" on Twitter. Look for people who have written about this beat in the last 6 months. They already care — you're giving them a new chapter in a story they're already telling.

**Target reporters and outlets:**
| Reporter/Outlet | Beat | Contact approach |
|----------------|------|-----------------|
| Brittny Mejia — LA Times | Immigrant communities in LA | @BrittnyMejia on Twitter/X, brittny.mejia@latimes.com |
| Cindy Carcamo — LA Times | Immigration, Latino communities | @cindycarcamo on Twitter |
| Lomi Kriel — ProPublica/LA Times | Immigration enforcement | @lomikriel on Twitter |
| Aura Bogado — formerly Reveal News | Environmental/civil rights, immigrant communities | Known for community-centered reporting |
| Univision Noticias LA | Spanish-language, massive Latino reach | Contact through univision.com/contacto-noticias |
| Telemundo 52 LA | Spanish-language local news | telemundo52.com — community stories desk |
| LAist (KPCC) | LA community journalism | tips@laist.com |
| Documented (documentedny.com) | Immigration news | Though NY-focused, national reach, tech-forward |
| NBC Latino | National Latino-focused stories | nbcnews.com/latino |

**Step 2: Build the pitch around a human story, not the product**
The worst pitch: "We built an app that..."
The best pitch: "I have a family in East LA who used this during a traffic stop last month. Can I tell you what happened?"

**Your pitch angle:**
- Lead with the detained US citizen angle — that's the most legally/emotionally powerful hook
- Mention the Latino GDP stat ($3.6T, 4th largest economy in the world)
- The product is the solution — the story is the problem

**Step 3: Make yourself quotable as an expert before the pitch**
Start posting on LinkedIn and Twitter now — not about the app, but about the problem. "Did you know that in [city], X number of US citizens have been detained?" becomes your quoted expertise. When a reporter covers this beat, they'll find you.

**Step 4: The soft launch press moment**
Time a small announcement with App Store approval. Draft a one-paragraph press release focused on the community story. Reach out to 5 reporters personally, not via blast. Give one outlet exclusivity for 48 hours.

**Sample pitch email (send to specific reporters, customize each):**
> Subject: A free app for the LA Latino community — one story you might want to tell
>
> [Reporter name] — I've been following your coverage of [specific story they wrote].
>
> I'm a 1st gen Mexican American and 2x founder. I just launched Acción — a free, anonymous safety app that lets you share your location with your family if you're stopped. No account, no data stored, completely private.
>
> The reason I built it: brown-skinned US citizens are being detained. The community doesn't have infrastructure for this moment. We do now.
>
> I have real users. I have a story from [beta tester, if they've consented]. I'm not looking for a product review — I'm looking for a reporter who wants to tell a community story. Would a 20-minute call be worth it?

**KPI:** One published story, any outlet, within 60 days of App Store launch.
