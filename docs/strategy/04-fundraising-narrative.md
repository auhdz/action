## The One-Liner

**Safety for la comunidad.**

## The Hook (First 30 Seconds of Any Pitch)

> "The 4th largest economy in the world has no safety infrastructure. The Latino community in the United States has a GDP of $3.6 trillion — bigger than Japan, bigger than Germany. 60 million people. And right now, brown-skinned Americans — citizens, with US passports — are being detained based on how they look. Their families have no way to know where they went. We're fixing that."

This hook does four things:
1. Reframes the market with a stat that surprises people (the GDP number)
2. Establishes the scale before naming the problem
3. Lands the moral injustice (US citizens — not a gray area)
4. Sets up the solution in one word: fixing

---

## The Problem

Brown people in America are being detained. Not hypothetically — it's documented, it's ongoing, and it's accelerating.

**The facts:**
- Under current enforcement, racial profiling is not a bug — federal courts have permitted it as a factor in determining "reasonable suspicion"
- Multiple US citizens have been detained and held for days before the error was corrected
- In LA County alone, there are 4+ million Latino residents — roughly 1 in 3 people in LA is Latino
- When a family member is stopped and detained, the family typically has no way to know: where they are, if they're safe, who to call, or what rights they have
- The Latino community has built informal networks — WhatsApp groups, Instagram accounts, community org alerts — to fill this gap. But it's fragmented, slow, and inaccessible in a crisis moment

**What currently exists:**
Nothing purpose-built for this. The closest tools — generic family tracking apps like Life360, or emergency apps like Citizen — were not built for this community, this problem, or this legal reality. They require accounts, collect data, and offer no protection in the moment that matters most.

---

## The Solution

Acción is a safety platform for the Latino community. Today, it's a mobile app. Tomorrow, it's a wearable.

**The app:**
- One button. Hold it for 3 seconds. Your trusted contacts get your live location instantly — via a web link, no app required on their end.
- Anonymous. No email. No name. No account that can be subpoenaed.
- Know Your Rights card in Spanish and English — what to say, what not to say, silently accessible in seconds.
- Community safety feed showing how neighbors feel about safety conditions in real time.
- Bilingual from day one — English and Spanish with one tap.

**The wearable (roadmap):**
A discrete BLE bracelet or pendant. Press once — SOS fires even when your phone is in your pocket or being held by someone else. The one moment the app can't serve you, the hardware does. This is the hardware goal. The app proves the demand. The hardware captures the premium market and creates a defensible moat.

## The Market

**Primary:** Latino and Hispanic community in the US
- 60+ million people
- Fastest-growing demographic in the United States
- Latino GDP: $3.6 trillion — if the US Latino community were a country, it would be the 4th largest economy in the world, surpassing Japan
- This is not a niche market. This is one of the most economically powerful demographic groups in the world with zero safety infrastructure built specifically for them.

**Secondary:** Allies — non-Latino family members, employers, attorneys, advocates, and community members who support and work alongside Latino families

**B2B adjacent:** Immigration law firms, nonprofit legal aid organizations, community health clinics, churches — all of whom serve this community daily and need tools to give their clients

## Why Now

Three forces have converged that didn't exist 5 years ago:

1. **Enforcement scale:** Federal immigration enforcement is at a historically unprecedented level. The fear is real, documented, and growing. This is not speculation — it's front-page news every week.

2. **Technology readiness:** Anonymous auth, Supabase Realtime, the ability to share a live GPS location via a web link with no app install — none of this was as accessible or affordable 3 years ago. We can build this for a fraction of what it would have cost.

3. **Community awakening:** The Latino community is more organized, more vocal, and more politically activated than at any point in modern history. The demand is there. The trust networks are there. The distribution infrastructure — through community orgs, churches, and social accounts — is there. We're not building the community. We're building the tool for a community that already exists.

## The Narrative Frame for Different Audiences

**For Latino angels and community-connected investors:**
Lead with the personal story. Lead with la comunidad. The GDP stat validates the market size after the emotional hook lands.

**For impact investors and grant officers:**
Lead with the civil liberties angle. US citizens being detained. Privacy-first design. Anonymous auth. The business model is secondary — the mission is primary.

**For tech-focused accelerators (YC, Techstars):**
Lead with the market size and the technical defensibility (anonymous, hardware roadmap, network effects from community map). Show the code is real. Show the architecture is thoughtful.

**For press:**
Lead with the human story. A family in East LA. A phone call that didn't come. One button that changes that. The GDP stat goes in paragraph 3 as the "why this matters at scale" moment.

## The Latino GDP Stat — How to Use It

The number: **$3.6 trillion** — the US Latino community's GDP as of 2024 (Stanford LACS, Latino Donor Collaborative annual report).

If the US Latino community were a country, it would be the **4th largest economy in the world** — larger than Japan ($4.2T for total Japan GDP — verify with most current year), approaching or surpassing depending on source and year.

**How to use it:**
- Not as a poverty statistic — as a power statistic
- "This isn't a charity case. This is the 4th largest economy in the world with zero dedicated safety infrastructure."
- It reframes the market from "underserved minority" to "underestimated superpower"
- It signals TAM in a way no demographic percentage ever does

**Where to cite it:**
- Latino Donor Collaborative (ldc.org) — publishes annual LiVE report on Latino economic contribution
- Stanford Center for Latin American Studies
- Use the most current version of the report when pitching

## Responses to Common Investor Objections

**"Can't Apple pull the app?"**
> We've been architected from day one to be App Store safe. The app is framed as personal safety and family communication — not law enforcement tracking. Our Know Your Rights feature shows public legal information. Our community feed shows neighborhood sentiment. If Apple removes us, we distribute via web app and TestFlight. But we're built to stay.

**"Isn't this too political?"**
> Safety isn't political. Knowing where your family member is when they don't come home isn't political. We don't take positions. We build tools. The community takes care of the rest.

**"What's the moat?"**
> Community trust. Hardware. Network effects from the community map. You cannot buy the trust of the Latino community — you earn it through proximity, quality, and time. We have the proximity. We're building the quality. The time advantage is already ours — no competitor is close.

**"What if enforcement changes and the urgency goes away?"**
> The hardware wearable is a general personal safety product. The community map is a general neighborhood safety product. If the specific immigration enforcement context changes, Acción becomes a general safety platform for Latino families. The $3.6T market doesn't go away. The fear of separation from family doesn't require a specific political context.

**"Why not a nonprofit?"**
> A nonprofit can't build a wearable supply chain. A nonprofit can't hire the engineering team to build real-time location infrastructure. A nonprofit can't pursue the B2B licensing path. We keep the product free for people who need it and build a business from those who can pay and from hardware. That's how we make this sustainable for the community long-term.
